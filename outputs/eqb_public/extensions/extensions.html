Payment shock and provincial panel

Pooled across regions the payment shock has the wrong sign; it matters in Ontario. Out of sample (origins every year from 2007, horizons 1-12) no model beats Ontario UR+HPI significantly, and every model misses the 2023-26 rise in Ontario arrears. Keep the champion; use the panel with the payment shock as a challenger and as the basis for a baseline renewal overlay.

Model-based scenarios (Bayesian VAR)

Scenarios are the median paths within severity bands of 3,000 simulated paths; probabilities are the band widths (upside 10%, baseline 40%, downside 40%, severe 10%). The VAR's severe band is much milder than the configured severe scenario: Gaussian shocks rarely produce a 2008- or 2020-sized jump, so the judgemental severe scenario sits well beyond the VAR's 1-in-40 tail. The VAR's house prices also drift up at their 1994-2026 average growth, faster than the configured baseline. Use the BVAR to check the baseline and the weights, not to replace the severe narrative.

Full-chain backtest against EQB's reported PCL

Over FY2020-FY2025 the model gives C$225m against C$363m reported (correlation 0.83). It is close in FY2022-23 and, given the actual proxy path, catches the 2021 release, but explains less than half of FY2024-25: EQB's own losses rose faster than the industry proxies the PDs are scaled to, and the proxy-scaling calibration cannot see that. This is the case for bank-specific default data (loan-level PD) or a management overlay.
