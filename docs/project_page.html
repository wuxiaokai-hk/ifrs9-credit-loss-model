IFRS 9 Credit-Loss Scenario Model

How much could a C$49bn loan book lose in a downturn, and how much should it provision? A scenario model that answers both, with an IFRS 9 expected-credit-loss engine, out-of-sample model validation and an interactive app. Built on EQB Inc.'s book at July 31, 2026, using only public data.

IFRS 9 stage engine

Loans migrate to Stage 2 as the recession deepens, then cure

Every quarter, the model moves gross loans between Stage 1 (12-month ECL), Stage 2 (significant increase in credit risk, lifetime ECL) and Stage 3 (credit-impaired), with cures, defaults and write-offs. Under the severe scenario more than half the book reaches Stage 2 at the trough.

Method

From a macro scenario to a provision

  1. Scenarios

    Four paths for unemployment, house prices, rates and GDP; the severe one is sized like the Bank of England's 2025 test and Ontario's early-1990s downturn. The app builds new ones from shocks.

  2. PD satellites

    For each segment, logit default rate on its lag and up to three macro drivers: screen, correlation clusters, a constrained search with sign priors and VIF limits, Bayesian model averaging, and Bank of Canada, Fed and ECB/EBA models re-estimated as challengers.

  3. Champion by out-of-sample record

    At every fourth quarter from 2006, the whole selection is re-run on data available then. The champion has correct signs, the lowest forecast error and a captured GFC within 80–200%; Diebold-Mariano tests compare it with an AR(1) benchmark.

  4. LGD from collateral

    Mortgage losses come from the loan-to-value at the time of sale on the scenario house-price path, net of selling costs and a distressed-sale discount; insured, Frye-Jacobs and fixed LGDs cover the other books.

  5. IFRS 9 measurement

    SICR against origination PD, 12-month and lifetime ECL discounted at the effective interest rate, a three-year forecast period with reversion, probability-weighted scenarios, overlays, and IFRS 7 roll-forwards and sensitivities.
